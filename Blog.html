Stephen DeAugustino

*Heroes of Metro Magic*

Week 1

I really enjoyed both writings and felt both a connection between them and to myself. Some of the concepts I parsed in each writing run parallel to the bigger ideas I will try to implement in my Visual Magic Project (Titled Heroes of Metro Magic... more on that later). Starting with In Defense of the Poor Image: As a rapidly aging Millennial that would like to believe I am nuanced with refined taste in media, reading this article made me realize that my earliest inspirations to make a livelihood of working in the field started through the consumption of the Poor Image; from recording Saturday morning cartoons recorded on old VHS tapes (with artifacts of the previous episodes still tracking through), to downloading pirated videos on Kazaa that usually contained surprise edits from whoever uploaded them, and playing old Flash games on websites banned on school browsers that most certainly only exist on InternetArchive. Without really knowing the concept, I am largely a product of Poor Images.

There’s a sense of longing that comes with that. Living in a post-internet era, the joy I found in consuming physical media and Web 1.0 growing up is hard to come by today. So rare are the simple experiences that come with being unconnected, because you simply can’t consume media without it being shared over a global-sized table. There are strengths to being as connected as we are today. Still, there are also vulnerabilities and threats to exposure, and strangely, reading An Account of Peach Blossom Spring conjured many of those feelings, despite being written roughly 1,500 years before the internet. Reading about the fisherman stumbling upon a village cut off from the rest of the world, maintaining a simpler way of life, felt like stumbling upon an old GeoCities site, totally intact from the doom-scrolling dread of the present.

It’s this sense of hauntology, the hunting of cultural memory, that I want to explore deeper in my project. While the direction and execution of it may change over the course of this class, I want to explore the search for joy in simple things amongst the chaos of the now; like a form of arcane magic long forgotten amongst the complexity of our current landscape, but still able to alchemize dark times into the most important gold: Joy.
